# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Argue about code, not people. Assume the person you're replying to is smart and
acting in good faith, because they usually are.

## What's expected

- **Critique the work, not the author.** "This has a race condition on redirect" is useful.
  "This is amateur code" is not, and it's also less informative.
- **Say what you actually think.** Politeness that hides a real objection wastes everyone's
  time. Disagree directly and specifically.
- **Be patient with people who are new.** Everyone was new. Someone asking a basic question
  about hooks or tree-sitter is doing the right thing by asking rather than guessing.
- **Report what happened, not what you wish had happened.** If your benchmark came out
  badly, or your PR doesn't fully close the issue, say so. That standard applies to
  contributors and maintainer alike.

## What isn't acceptable

Harassment, personal attacks, discrimination on any basis, sustained disruption, publishing
someone's private information, or unwelcome sexual attention. Also: submitting work you
didn't write as your own, or vendoring code from another project when this repo's clean-room
principle says implement it fresh.

## Scope

Applies in issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages and code review — anywhere
people interact around this project.

## Enforcement

Report to the maintainer through GitHub, or privately via the contact route in
`SECURITY.md` if it's sensitive. Reports are handled confidentially.

Consequences scale with severity: a private word for a first lapse, a public correction for
something that affected others, removal for repeated or serious behaviour. The maintainer
decides, and will say plainly why.
